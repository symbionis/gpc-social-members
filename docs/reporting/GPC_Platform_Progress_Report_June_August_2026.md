# Every Guest Has a Name

**Platform Progress Report — June to August 2026**
Prepared for Thierry &amp; the Geneva Polo Club team

---

## The Big Picture

The last report covered the platform learning to run the club's events: pages, ticket sales,
a member area, communications. It worked, but it counted **seats**. A booking for four was
four places against a cap — the club knew who paid, and nothing about the other three.

Since June the platform has learned to count **people**. Every ticket now belongs to a named
person with their own code for the door. Guests receive their own ticket rather than a
forwarded screenshot, can add their name, correct a spelling, upgrade, cancel, or sign the
waiver before they arrive. The door scans a code and shows who just walked in.

That one change runs through everything below: the door, the guest list, the waiting list,
refunds and the finance view all rest on knowing who is coming rather than how many.

Ten events ran on it in eleven weeks.

---

## 1. Every Ticket Belongs to a Named Person

**Before:** a booking recorded the buyer and a number. Four places meant one name and three
strangers. Guests arrived as "+3", and the club had no way to reach them before or after.

**Now:** every seat is a ticket with a holder.

- **Names are required at checkout** — first and last name for every place, not just the buyer's.
- **Each guest gets their own ticket by email**, with their own code. No forwarding a screenshot
and hoping.
- **Household bookings stay together** — a family or couple booking on one email receives one
message containing each person's ticket.
- **Guests can fix their own details** — a misspelt name or a wrong email is corrected by the
guest, not by an administrator.
- **No code, no bracelet** — stated plainly in the ticket email, so guests arrive expecting to be
scanned.

> **What changed for the club:** of 543 tickets issued this period, 478 carry a named holder.
> Those are people the club can recognise at the gate, seat correctly, and contact afterwards —
> where before there was a number.

---

## 2. The Door Runs on Scans

**Before:** door staff worked from a printed list or a phone, matching names by eye and marking
people off by hand. Busy gates meant queues and guesswork.

**Now:** a check-in console built for standing outside in the sun.

- **Scan to admit** — the guest's code is scanned and the screen says who they are and whether
they are expected.
- **A live arrivals list** — who has come in, updating as it happens.
- **The waiver on screen, full size**, signed at the gate by anyone who has not already signed.
- **Guest lists shown separately**, grouped by the member who invited them.
- **A printed sheet as a fallback** — an A–Z roster for doors worked by hand, with leads in bold
and their guests indented beneath.
- **Cancelled places no longer appear** — a refunded seat cannot be admitted by mistake.

> **Time saved:** the door no longer needs a person cross-referencing a list. 180 arrivals were
> recorded by scan this period, each one landing in the club's records at the moment it happened.

---

## 3. Guests Manage Their Own Booking

**Before:** every change went through an administrator. A name to correct, a guest swapped, an
extra place wanted — all of it arrived by message and was done by hand.

**Now:** each booking has its own page, and so does each ticket.

- **Name or rename a place**, up to the moment of the event.
- **See every ticket in the booking**, with each person's code.
- **Sign the waiver in advance**, so the gate is quicker.
- **Cancel a place**, which frees the seat for someone else immediately.

> **Time saved:** the administrator is no longer the route through which every small change
> passes. Corrections that used to be a message and a manual edit now happen without anyone at
> the club being involved.

---

## 4. Buying More, and Moving Up

**Before:** a guest who wanted an extra place, or a better ticket, had to book again separately
or ask for an invoice.

**Now:** both happen from the booking itself.

- **Add places to an existing booking** — paid for immediately, tickets issued at once.
- **Upgrade a ticket type** and pay only the difference.
- **One price everywhere** — the rate a person sees when booking, adding, or upgrading comes from
a single source, so a member rate stays a member rate throughout.
- **An itemised receipt** on the confirmation, showing what was bought at what price.

---

## 5. The Waiting List Now Sells

**Before:** the waiting list collected names. When a place freed up, someone had to notice,
choose a person, write to them, and take payment separately.

**Now:** the club sends a paid offer.

- **Offer a place to a named person** on the list, with a link that lets them pay and claim it.
- **The offer is theirs alone** — it cannot be forwarded and used by someone else.
- **The system refuses to over-promise** — offers are blocked when the event is full, with a
warning before an administrator can create one anyway.

> **What changed for the club:** a returned ticket becomes revenue instead of an empty seat, and
> the sequence runs without anyone tracking who was asked first.

---

## 6. Guest Lists and Complimentary Places

**Before:** complimentary guests were a workaround — a free booking made by hand, with no record
of who invited whom.

**Now:** guest lists are a proper part of an event.

- **Add a guest list against an event**, with places allocated to named guests.
- **The door sees them grouped by sponsor**, so it is clear who invited whom.
- **The roster shows how each seat was obtained** — bought, comped, or from the guest list.
- **The complimentary share is visible** in the event overview, rather than hidden inside the
headcount.

---

## 7. Money in One Place

**Before:** income was visible as individual payments. Working out what an event made, or what a
member had paid, meant assembling it by hand.

**Now:** a finance area, with its own access level so a treasurer can see the money without
seeing everything else.

- **Membership and event income together**, by month and by season.
- **Refunds issued from the platform**, and deducted from the figures rather than sitting outside
them.
- **Cancellations release the seat immediately** and are recorded as refundable.
- **Referral credit per originator**, with a monthly breakdown and a link through to the payment
behind each figure.
- **Date paid** shown against each member.

> **What changed for the club:** 110 paid bookings and CHF 14,185 in event income this period,
> visible in one place rather than reconstructed from receipts.

---

## 8. Less to Go Wrong

Not everything worth doing adds a feature. Four things were removed this period, each of which
had been a source of confusion or mistakes.

- **Children's tickets** — a separate ticket type with its own rules and its own gate at the
door. Every ticket now carries a name and a waiver regardless of age, which is simpler and
safer.
- **Ticket forwarding** — passing a ticket to someone else was a second way of doing what naming
a guest already does, and it produced tickets nobody could account for.
- **Public self-check-in** — guests checking themselves in from their phone meant arrivals the
door had not seen. Check-in is now the door's job alone.
- **Two ways to buy** — buying a ticket and adding one to an existing booking followed separate
paths. They now share one, so a change to pricing or rules applies to both.

> **What changed for the club:** fewer paths through the same task means fewer ways for a guest
> to end up in a state nobody planned for.

---

## 9. Behind the Scenes

Work that never appears on a screen, and matters most when it is absent.

- **Deploys check themselves.** Errors in the live platform now report with enough detail to find
the cause, rather than a line number in compressed code.
- **The build no longer depends on an outside service** being available at the moment it runs.
- **Signing in works with iPhone's code autofill**, which had been silently failing.
- **Twenty-one database changes** that had been applied to the live system without being recorded
were recovered and written down, so the platform's history is complete again.

## What This Sets Up

Every improvement above rests on the same foundation: the club now knows, by name, who came to
what. That is the raw material for the next stage — following up with the guests who attended,
understanding how many of them join, and turning a crowd into members rather than a headcount.

The roadmap covers that ground separately.
